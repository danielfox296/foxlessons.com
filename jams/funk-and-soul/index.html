Jams · Funk and soul

Funk and soul jams on the Front Range

Funk and soul jams run on a groove more than on chord changes. Most tunes sit on one or two chords, and the rhythm section locks together underneath them.

Schedules change. Confirm with the venue or host before you go.

How funk and soul nights work

What gets played

Expect vamps on one or two chords, James Brown and Meters tunes, Stevie Wonder and Aretha Franklin songs, and neo-soul grooves. On the nights that lean hip-hop, rappers freestyle over a groove the band keeps going.

How signup works

Signup is often looser than at a blues night. Some nights keep a list, and on others the host waves players up, often calling singers and horn players in batches. Check the card for that night's signup.

How a set goes

The leader sets a groove and the band locks into it. Tunes run long, players solo over the vamp, and the leader cues breakdowns, hits and the ending with hand signals or a shout.

Habits worth knowing

  • Play less than you want to, because the groove needs space.
  • Watch the leader the whole time, since breakdowns and endings come from hand signals.
  • Ask what the cues mean before you go up. A raised fist, a raised hand and a spin each mean something different.
  • If you lose the downbeat, drop out for a bar and come back in. Playing against the groove sounds worse than a bar of silence.

What each instrumentalist needs

The skill level on each card links here. Find your instrument, then the level that matches the night.

  • 1 to 2, first-timer friendly Your first time on any stage is fine, and so is playing basic chords or a groove and following along.
  • 3, middle You hold your part, take a solo or a verse, and know the common tunes.
  • 4 to 5, experienced People expect you to know the tunes by name and keep up, and working pros sit in.

All five levels, defined

Guitarists

1 to 2, first-timer friendly
You can play a clean ninth-chord vamp with a sixteenth-note scratch strum at one volume, and stay off the low strings.
3, middle
You can play single-note riffs that lock with the bass, keep dotted rhythms from rushing, and comp through a two-chord soul tune.
4 to 5, experienced
You can switch between rhythm parts and lead fills, play Motown and Stax voicings, and keep a sixteenth-note part steady for ten minutes without drifting.

We build the right-hand feel and the two or three voicings that cover most of the songbook. Guitar lessons

Bassists

1 to 2, first-timer friendly
You can keep a one-bar groove going for as long as the tune lasts, mostly on the root and locked to the kick drum.
3, middle
You can play syncopated sixteenth-note lines with ghost notes, stay with the kick drum through a breakdown, and play a slap groove or two at real tempos.
4 to 5, experienced
You can sit in the pocket at any tempo, play fills that come back to the downbeat, catch cued hits in the middle of a groove, and anchor a ten-minute vamp without missing the ending.

We lock in sixteenth-note feel against real tracks and practice reading cues in the middle of a groove. Bass lessons

Drummers

1 to 2, first-timer friendly
You can play a solid backbeat at a medium tempo, save fills until the leader asks for them, and count in without rushing.
3, middle
You can play sixteenth-note hi-hat grooves with ghosted snare notes, play James Brown and Meters feels, and catch cued breaks.
4 to 5, experienced
You can play displaced grooves and half-time shuffles, bring the volume from a whisper up on cue, and drive a breakdown back to the full band cleanly.

We drill the funk feels and a steady backbeat, then practice reading cues. Drum lessons

Keyboard players

1 to 2, first-timer friendly
You can play a two-chord vamp on an electric piano sound with short stabs in time, and leave the low end to the bass.
3, middle
You can play clavinet-style rhythm parts, use seventh and ninth voicings, swell an organ behind a singer, and take a solo that stays inside the groove.
4 to 5, experienced
You can split parts between organ and electric piano, cover the bass with your left hand when there's no bassist, and arrange pads, stabs and fills so the groove keeps its space.

We get the voicings and the rhythm-hand feel down, and practice playing inside a band without covering it up. Piano lessons

Singers

1 to 2, first-timer friendly
You know one soul tune in your key with the form memorized, and you're comfortable repeating a chorus while the band vamps.
3, middle
You can ad-lib over the groove, bring the band down for a verse and up for a chorus, and sing backgrounds behind another singer.
4 to 5, experienced
You can front the band, call breakdowns, stack background parts on the spot, teach the crowd a hook, and end a vamp on cue.

Inquire about a good teacher for you, and I'll recommend someone if I know one.

Horn players

1 to 2, first-timer friendly
You can play single-note stabs in time, sit out when the part calls for it, and move one riff to any key.
3, middle
You can play in a section with another horn, play the common funk lines and backgrounds behind vocals, and take a short solo that builds.
4 to 5, experienced
You can write a line on the spot and teach it to the other horns between choruses, solo over one chord without running out of ideas, and follow the dynamics on cue.

Inquire about a good teacher for you, and I'll recommend someone if I know one.

Harmonica players

1 to 2, first-timer friendly
You can play riffs in second position over a one-chord vamp, play sparingly, and lower the mic when you're not playing.
3, middle
You can play chugging rhythms locked with the drums, play fills between vocal lines, and cup a tone that doesn't feed back.
4 to 5, experienced
You can play third-position minor vamps, play section-style lines with the horns, and build a solo on the groove more than on the chord changes.

Inquire about a good teacher for you, and I'll recommend someone if I know one.

Walk in ready.

In a lesson or two before your first night, we cover the grooves and tunes those nights run on, how the signup works, and how a set goes. Once you're my student, I'll help you pick the right night, and I'll be there.
