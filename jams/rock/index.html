Jams · Rock

Rock jams on the Front Range

Rock jams run from classic covers to Grateful Dead nights where one tune can last half an hour. You'll need to know the songs, or know how to listen your way into one.

Schedules change. Confirm with the venue or host before you go.

How rock nights work

What gets played

On most nights players call classic covers everyone can join, like the Beatles, the Rolling Stones, Tom Petty, Led Zeppelin and Creedence. Grateful Dead and jam-band nights stretch tunes into long improvisations, and the metal and indie nights work more like an open mic with gear provided.

How signup works

Most nights keep a list near the stage. Some hosts collect names and instruments and build the bands, and others let a band that arrives together take a slot. Check the card's signup line for how your night works.

How a set goes

Each group plays two to four songs. Someone calls a song and a key, the band finds the form fast, and whoever gets the nod takes the solo. At Grateful Dead nights the band moves from song to song and decides the transitions on stage.

Habits worth knowing

  • Call songs with four chords and a chorus everyone knows. Strangers can't follow a deep cut on the first try.
  • Agree on the ending before the count-in, whether that's one last chorus or riding the riff until the drummer stops.
  • Tune before you plug in.
  • Match the stage volume and let the sound system make you loud enough.

What each instrumentalist needs

The skill level on each card links here. Find your instrument, then the level that matches the night.

  • 1 to 2, first-timer friendly Your first time on any stage is fine, and so is playing basic chords or a groove and following along.
  • 3, middle You hold your part, take a solo or a verse, and know the common tunes.
  • 4 to 5, experienced People expect you to know the tunes by name and keep up, and working pros sit in.

All five levels, defined

Guitarists

1 to 2, first-timer friendly
You can play open and barre chords, use a capo if you need one, and strum a four-chord song in time all the way through.
3, middle
You can play in the common keys without a capo, play pentatonic leads, remember the riffs from the classic songbook, and switch between rhythm and lead when someone nods at you.
4 to 5, experienced
You can change feels in the middle of a tune, build a solo, leave the other guitarist space the way Grateful Dead players do, and lead a band through a song you called.

We build the cover-night songbook and your lead playing, and we practice passing parts around a band. Guitar lessons

Bassists

1 to 2, first-timer friendly
You can play root notes through a four-chord progression, locked to the kick drum, with your eyes on the guitarist's left hand for the changes.
3, middle
You can walk into choruses with passing tones, play the bass lines everybody expects on the classics, and keep a jam steady while the guitars wander.
4 to 5, experienced
You can play in any key, follow key changes on the fly, lead the Grateful Dead and jam-band passages where the bass takes over, and pull a drifting band back to the downbeat.

We cover a rock bassist's job song by song, and we practice following chord changes you've never seen. Bass lessons

Drummers

1 to 2, first-timer friendly
You can play a straight backbeat at one tempo, put crashes where they belong, and skip any fill you can't finish cleanly.
3, middle
You can play the common feels, straight eighths, half-time, train beats and 12/8, count in at the right tempo, and end songs either cold or with a long ringing finish.
4 to 5, experienced
You can improvise with the band's dynamics the way Grateful Dead drummers do, keep odd phrase lengths without losing the band, and steer a twenty-minute jam from the kit.

We lock in a steady tempo and the common feels, then practice cueing and endings. Drum lessons

Keyboard players

1 to 2, first-timer friendly
You can play triads and suspended chords through a four-chord song, play pads under the vocals, and keep your hands out of the low octaves.
3, middle
You can play organ pads and fills, drive the rockers with piano eighth notes, read a guitarist's hands for the changes, and take a solo that peaks and ends.
4 to 5, experienced
You know B3 organ language and Grateful Dead and Allman Brothers voicings, you can play synth leads where they fit, and you can cover the melody when there's no singer.

We sort out the sounds and the roles, and we practice following chord changes nobody rehearsed. Piano lessons

Singers

1 to 2, first-timer friendly
You know one classic song all the way through, in your key, and you're ready to count it off yourself.
3, middle
You have a handful of covers in different feels, you can find your pitch over a loud stage, and you can signal the ending so the band stops with you.
4 to 5, experienced
You can pick a set list for any band, sing harmonies on the spot, and make a band of strangers sound rehearsed.

Inquire about a good teacher for you, and I'll recommend someone if I know one.

Horn players

1 to 2, first-timer friendly
You can play roots and riffs in the guitar keys, sit out until your solo, and play one phrase you can fit anywhere.
3, middle
You know the classic horn lines, you can transpose quickly, and you play backgrounds that stay clear of the vocal.
4 to 5, experienced
You can arrange a two-horn section live, solo over a one-chord jam, and match a loud guitar without overblowing.

Inquire about a good teacher for you, and I'll recommend someone if I know one.

Harmonica players

1 to 2, first-timer friendly
You can play in first and second position over G, C and D, and you can tell when a song doesn't want harmonica at all.
3, middle
You can play folk-rock phrases, use a neck rack if you also strum, use hand effects, and play short fills between vocal lines.
4 to 5, experienced
You can get an amplified tone for the blues-rock numbers, pick positions for minor keys, and solo without chasing the guitars.

Inquire about a good teacher for you, and I'll recommend someone if I know one.

Walk in ready.

In a lesson or two before your first night, we cover the covers and keys those nights lean on, how the signup works, and how a set goes. Once you're my student, I'll help you pick the right night, and I'll be there.
