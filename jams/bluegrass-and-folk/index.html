Jams · Bluegrass and folk

Bluegrass and folk jams on the Front Range

Bluegrass, old-time, Irish and folk jams mostly happen in a circle, acoustic and unplugged, on tunes players learn by ear. Players care about etiquette here more than at any other kind of jam on this list.

Schedules change. Confirm with the venue or host before you go.

How bluegrass and folk jams work

What gets played

Players in the circle play bluegrass standards like Salt Creek and Nine Pound Hammer, old-time fiddle tunes repeated until everyone has them, Irish session sets, and folk songs with choruses the whole circle can sing. Most tunes are in G, A, D and C, and capos are welcome.

How signup works

Most circles have no signup. You sit in the circle and play, the lead moves around the circle, and you can pass when it's your turn. Some jams split into a fast circle and a slow one, and the slow circle is the place to start.

How a set goes

Whoever's turn it is calls a tune and a key and starts it. In bluegrass, solos, called breaks, go around the circle in order. In old-time and Irish circles everyone plays the melody together and nobody solos. A tune ends when someone raises a foot or calls the last time through.

Habits worth knowing

  • Match the circle's volume and tempo. If you can't hear the fiddle, you're playing too loud.
  • Play rhythm on tunes you don't know yet, and take the melody only after you've heard it come around a few times.
  • In old-time and Irish circles, play the tune with everyone and save improvising for bluegrass.
  • Ask before you record, and ask a tune's name after it ends.

What each instrumentalist needs

  • 1 to 2, first-timer friendly Your first time on any stage is fine, and so is playing basic chords or a groove and following along.
  • 3, middle You hold your part, take a solo or a verse, and know the common tunes.
  • 4 to 5, experienced People expect you to know the tunes by name and keep up, and working pros sit in.

All five levels, defined

Guitarists

1 to 2, first-timer friendly
You can play G, C, D and A with a capo, keep a boom-chuck rhythm, and follow the changes by watching the other players' hands.
3, middle
You can play solid rhythm at fiddle-tune tempo with bass runs between chords, take a flatpicked break on the common tunes, and play the G run where it belongs.
4 to 5, experienced
You can take breaks at any tempo that gets called, crosspick, back a singer with no mandolin chop to lean on, and kick tunes off cleanly.

We put rhythm first, since every circle needs it, and build breaks from there. Guitar lessons

Bassists

1 to 2, first-timer friendly
You can play root-fifth in G, A and D on beats one and three, every time.
3, middle
You can walk into chord changes, play waltz time, read a capoed guitarist's chord shapes, and keep the tempo steady against a rushing banjo.
4 to 5, experienced
You can play in any key that gets called, slap where an old-time jam wants it, and keep time for the whole circle.

We make root-fifth automatic and train your ear for the changes in the fiddle keys. Bass lessons

Banjo players

1 to 2, first-timer friendly
You can play rolls in G at an easy tempo, vamp on the tunes you don't know, and capo up for A.
3, middle
You can play Scruggs-style breaks on the standards, play backup up the neck, play basic clawhammer for the old-time circles, and take your turn without speeding up.
4 to 5, experienced
You can take breaks at session tempo in any key that gets called, play melodic style where it fits, and switch between bluegrass drive and old-time rhythm.

Inquire about a good teacher for you, and I'll recommend someone if I know one.

Mandolin players

1 to 2, first-timer friendly
You can play two-finger chords for G, C, D and A, chop on the offbeat, and use tremolo only when you choose to.
3, middle
You can take breaks on the common fiddle tunes, play double stops, kick off a tune cleanly, and keep the chop going when the guitar takes a break.
4 to 5, experienced
You know a deep list of fiddle tunes, play at session tempo in any key that gets called, pick up old-time and Irish melodies by ear, and lead tunes from the chop.

Inquire about a good teacher for you, and I'll recommend someone if I know one.

Fiddle players

1 to 2, first-timer friendly
You can play first-position melodies in D, G and A with long, in-tune strokes, and play softly under the circle until you have the melody.
3, middle
You know the common session tunes from memory, play double stops and drones, kick off at a tempo you can keep, and play pads behind singers.
4 to 5, experienced
You can play bluegrass, old-time and Irish sets, take breaks at any tempo, add harmony lines on the spot, and lead sets without losing the circle.

Inquire about a good teacher for you, and I'll recommend someone if I know one.

Singers

1 to 2, first-timer friendly
You know one folk song with a chorus everyone knows, in a key the circle can play, with the words memorized.
3, middle
You can lead the verses while the circle sings the choruses, teach a chorus by singing it twice, and pitch the song so the last verse still fits your voice.
4 to 5, experienced
You can sing harmony above or below a lead on the spot, stack duet and trio parts, lead call and response, and reach the back of the venue with no microphone.

Inquire about a good teacher for you, and I'll recommend someone if I know one.

Walk in ready.

In a lesson or two before your first jam, we cover the fiddle keys and circle tunes that come around, how the signup works, and how a set goes. Once you're my student, I'll help you pick the right jam, and I'll be there.
