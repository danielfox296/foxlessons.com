What's the best instrument for a kid with ADHD?

There's no best instrument for ADHD, only a best fit for your kid. Here is how to find it without buying the wrong thing twice.

Daniel Fox Denver · 2026-08-03 · 3 min read

There is no single best instrument for ADHD, and the lists that rank them are guessing. What matters is fit, meaning the instrument your kid is actually pulled toward, played on music your family actually listens to. That’s what the dropout research supports, and it’s how I teach.

The short version for a busy parent: follow the pull, rent before you buy where you can, and talk to the teacher before spending anything.

Why fit beats ranking #

The ranked lists get two things right. Movement helps a lot of ADHD kids, and instruments differ in how immediately they reward you. Those are real considerations. A kid who needs to move may do better standing behind a drum kit or a bass than sitting still on a bench. A kid who needs to hear results now may light up on an instrument that sounds good on day one.

What the lists miss is that interest is what keeps a kid playing. The best documented predictors of a kid quitting are slow visible progress and lessons built on music that doesn’t match what the family listens to at home. A 2017 dropout study found exactly that pattern: kids taught a repertoire foreign to their own house walk away. The instrument your kid keeps air-drumming is a better bet than the instrument a list recommends.

What does the evidence actually say? #

Three things. Music training is among the better-evidenced activities for building attention and executive function skills in children generally. ADHD-specific studies, especially around rhythm and drumming, are promising but small. And one thing isn’t in dispute: interest-driven, movement-rich, immediate-feedback activity is exactly what an ADHD kid engages with, and playing an instrument is all three at once.

What I won’t tell you is that music fixes ADHD, and nobody should tell you that.

What if my kid wants drums and you want piano? #

This gap is real and documented. Surveys of lesson families find parents lean piano while kids lean guitar and drums. Most teachers can only serve one side of it.

I teach both sides, bass, guitar, drums, and piano. If the drums are the pull, we start there and the piano conversation stays open. Some families run both, one kid on each, back to back in the same visit. Starting where the pull is costs you nothing, because the habit of loving practice transfers between instruments, and starting where the pull isn’t can cost you the whole experiment.

How do lessons work for an ADHD kid? #

Lessons happen in your home, in a room your kid already knows, with a parent or guardian home during the lesson. Kids’ lessons run 45 minutes, built in short varied segments. Movement is normal. We work by ear and chord charts before any paper, on songs your kid picked. Lights, volume, and breaks flex to what your kid needs, and a blank practice week gets problem-solving instead of a lecture.

I’m a music teacher, not a music therapist. My job is helping you play. If you’re looking for clinical goals with music, Denver has board-certified music therapists, and I’m glad to point you toward them. If you’re weighing the two, I wrote a plain guide: music lessons or music therapy.

What should we buy? #

Nothing yet. Talk to me before you buy anything. I’ll help you find the right instrument for your budget without overspending. For band instruments, renting first is often the right move, and for drums there are quiet starting setups that keep the household peace.

References

  1. Gerelus and Comeau (2017), University of Ottawa piano-student dropout study: rate of visible progress and repertoire mismatched to the home's music culture predict quitting.
  2. Systematic review (2022) and meta-analysis (2025) of music training and executive function in children ages 3 to 12: attention and inhibition gains in general child populations.
  3. Guitar Center and OnePoll lesson-family surveys: parents prefer piano while kids prefer electric guitar and drums.
